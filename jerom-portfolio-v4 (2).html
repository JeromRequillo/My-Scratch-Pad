02 — Beyond Work

Get to Know Me

A few things you won't find on a resume, but explain a lot about how I teach.

I still fix engines on weekends
Old habits stick — tinkering with small engines keeps my hands and my problem-solving sharp outside the classroom.
I'm happiest near open water
Years spent on marine outboards left me with a permanent soft spot for boats, tides, and anything that runs on an engine near the sea.
I talk with my hands — a lot
Ask any former student. Explaining things visually, even mid-air, is just how my brain works.
I've changed careers three times
Automotive parts, marine repair, then teaching. Each one taught me something the last one couldn't.
Coffee first, lesson plans second
No exceptions. My students learned this about me faster than they learned the actual lesson.
My dream classroom has a workbench in it
If I ever get to design my own classroom, half of it is whiteboard and the other half is a workshop.

05 — Philosophy

Teaching Philosophy

Education is not about filling a bucket, but lighting a fire.

I believe in fostering a student-centered learning environment where curiosity is ignited, critical thinking is developed, and real-world applications are emphasized. Drawing from my engineering background and hands-on experience, I integrate practical problem-solving into lessons to prepare students for future challenges.

Education is not just about imparting knowledge — it's about building character, resilience, and a lifelong love for learning. I strive to create inclusive classrooms where every student feels valued and empowered to succeed.
